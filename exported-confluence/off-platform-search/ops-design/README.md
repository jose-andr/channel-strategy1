## Purpose of this phase

The Design phase creates the future-state Off-Platform Search approach, patterns, artefacts and test plan needed to improve how customers find accurate, current and useful City of Melbourne information before they reach owned channels.

The goal is to move from an agreed off-platform search problem and scope into a practical, testable approach that City of Melbourne can influence through source quality, content structure, metadata, redirects, monitoring, escalation and governance.

This phase should answer:

**Do we have a testable off-platform search approach that helps customers find accurate, current and useful City of Melbourne information before they reach owned channels?**

---

## Design question

**What should the future off-platform search approach look like, and how will City of Melbourne improve source quality, findability, accuracy, risk controls and monitoring across Google, AI-generated answers, third-party sources and owned content?**

## Channel and team roles

| Channel / team | Role in the future state | Key requirements | Risks / considerations |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Google search | External entry point that influences whether customers reach the right City source | Clear titles, metadata, page quality, redirects and authoritative content | City cannot directly control rankings or snippets |
| AI tools | External answer layer that may summarise City service information | Clear, current, structured and authoritative source material | Outputs vary and may not cite sources |
| Website | Primary City-owned destination for public service information and transactions | Clear page structure, customer task language and next steps | Outdated or duplicate pages can confuse results |
| Search / SEO capability | Supports findability and technical improvements | Metadata, redirects, indexing, schema and performance advice | Capacity and tooling constraints may limit speed |
| Content team | Improves page clarity, structure and currency | Content updates, plain English, review cycles | Content may depend on service owner approval |
| Knowledge team | Aligns source-of-truth and approved answers | Reusable knowledge, ownership and review alignment | Unclear source ownership may reduce quality |
| Service owners | Validate accuracy, risk and operational rules | Approve authoritative information and escalation guidance | Limited capacity may delay changes |
| Data / analytics | Tracks findability, contact impact and trend data | Search, web, CRM and Contact Centre data | Attribution may be imperfect |
| Risk / Legal / Privacy | Reviews higher-risk or regulated scenarios | Controls, disclaimers and escalation guidance | May require formal approval for high-risk changes |

## Controls and compliance considerations

| Area | Consideration | Required action | Owner |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Privacy | Search and AI answers should not encourage customers to disclose personal information insecurely | Make secure pathways and assisted support clear |  |
| Consent | Some service scenarios may require consent before action or advice | Identify where consent guidance is needed |  |
| Records | Content decisions, redirects and retirement actions may need traceability | Maintain decision log and source change record |  |
| Accessibility | Search result language and destination pages should support inclusive access | Use plain English, accessible headings and assisted pathways |  |
| Language and inclusion | Search terms and customer questions may vary across communities | Consider alternative terms, translated content and non-digital pathways |  |
| Information security | Some staff-only or restricted information should not be exposed | Separate public guidance from restricted operational detail |  |
| Data governance | Search and analytics data may be incomplete or variable | Record test conditions and data limitations |  |
| Legal / regulatory | Some advice must remain accurate to legislation, permits, payments or compliance requirements | Validate high-risk content with appropriate owners |  |
| AI safety | AI answers can be inaccurate, incomplete or variable | Use risk-based monitoring and avoid treating AI output as controlled channel content |  |

## Design exit criteria

Design can move into Deliver when:

- the future-state off-platform search concept is clear and linked to the defined problem
- priority search scenarios, search terms and AI prompts are documented
- authoritative City sources are identified for priority scenarios
- source ownership and review needs are documented
- content, metadata, redirect or retirement requirements are clear
- search improvement patterns are documented
- AI answer risks and controls are documented where relevant
- third-party source issues are assigned, monitored or parked with rationale
- current-state fixes are separated from future-state, dependent or monitoring items
- accessibility, plain language, privacy, records, legal, information security and AI safety considerations are visible
- key assumptions have a test plan or have been tested
- success measures are ready to be tracked
- there is enough detail to pilot, test, implement or hand over

**Do we have a testable off-platform search approach that helps customers find accurate, current and useful City of Melbourne information before they reach owned channels?**

If the answer is partial or no, the team should identify the specific design gaps, risks or decisions that need to be resolved before moving forward.
