## Purpose of this phase

The Define phase turns Off-Platform Search discovery evidence into a clear problem frame, agreed scope, success measures and priority opportunities.

The goal is to make sure the team understands the off-platform search problem before moving into Design.

This phase should answer:

**Do we have the right off-platform search problem, scope, risk framing and success measures to move forward into Design?**

---

## Define question

**What off-platform search problem are we solving, for whom, and what needs to be true for customers to find accurate, current and useful City of Melbourne information before they reach owned channels?**

## Off-platform search principles

These principles should guide the next stage of work.

| Principle | What it means for Off-Platform Search |
| --- | --- |
| Customer task first | Search scenarios should reflect what customers actually type or ask, not internal service names only |
| Authority matters | City-owned, current and approved sources should be easier to identify and trust |
| Accuracy before visibility | Higher ranking is not useful if the answer is wrong, incomplete or outdated |
| Risk-based prioritisation | High-risk, high-demand or regulated services should be prioritised before low-risk optimisation |
| Search is a channel | Google, AI tools and third-party sources shape the customer experience before owned channels |
| Source-of-truth before optimisation | Content must be current, owned and structured before search or AI performance can be improved sustainably |
| Evidence before preference | Design decisions should be based on search results, AI outputs, analytics and customer language evidence |
| Accessible and inclusive by design | Search result titles, snippets and destination pages should support plain English, assistive technology, language needs and non-digital pathways |
| Reuse before reinvention | Search findings should strengthen IA, KMS, content and channel routing work |
| Safe and compliant by design | Privacy, legal, records, information security and customer harm risks should be considered early |

## Search and AI risk framing

Use this section to classify issues before Design.

| Risk level | Meaning | Example | Design implication |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Low | Incorrect or unclear result is inconvenient but unlikely to cause harm | Low-risk general information is hard to find | Improve title, heading, metadata or content clarity |
| Medium | Incorrect or unclear result may cause wasted effort, avoidable contact or wrong pathway selection | Customer follows outdated process or contacts wrong team | Prioritise content fix, redirect or channel guidance |
| High | Incorrect or unclear result may cause financial, legal, safety, privacy or compliance impact | AI answer gives wrong advice for permit, enforcement, payment or regulated service | Escalate, apply controls, improve source-of-truth content and assign owner |
| Unknown | Risk cannot be assessed with available evidence | Source or answer is unclear, untested or variable | Gather more evidence before progressing |

## Constraints

| Constraint | Impact | Design implication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Policy / regulatory | Some service information may require formal wording or legal accuracy | Use plain English summaries with links to approved formal terms |
| Privacy / consent | Search results and AI answers should not encourage unsafe disclosure of personal information | Make secure and assisted pathways clear |
| Records | Content decisions, redirects and source-of-truth changes may need traceability | Record decisions, ownership and change rationale |
| Accessibility | Search result content and destination pages must support inclusive access | Use plain English, accessible page structure and assisted pathways |
| Technology | Search engines and AI tools are outside City control | Focus on source quality, metadata, content structure, redirects and monitoring |
| Data quality | Search and AI outputs vary by time, location, browser, account and tool version | Record test conditions and use repeatable test scripts |
| Content governance | Outdated or duplicate content may return if ownership is unclear | Define content owners, review cycles and retirement rules |
| Operational capacity | Content, SEO and service owner capacity may limit what can be fixed quickly | Prioritise high-risk and high-demand scenarios |
| Third-party dependency | External sites may publish outdated or incomplete information | Identify escalation, correction or monitoring approach |
| Governance | Without ongoing ownership, off-platform issues may reappear | Design minimum viable governance and monitoring model |

## Define exit criteria

Define can move into Design when:

- the off-platform search problem statement is clear and evidence-based
- priority customer tasks, services and search scenarios are agreed
- in-scope and out-of-scope items are documented
- priority opportunities are selected using customer impact, risk and feasibility
- success measures are defined
- source-of-truth issues and owners are visible
- search and AI risk levels are documented
- current-state fixes are separated from future-state, dependent or monitoring items
- content, SEO, IA and KMS dependencies are visible
- privacy, records, accessibility, legal and information security considerations are included where relevant
- open decisions have owners
- assumptions are documented
- there is enough clarity to create search improvement patterns, content recommendations, metadata changes, monitoring approach or source-of-truth improvements

## Final Define outcome

By the end of Define, the team should have a clear and agreed view of the Off-Platform Search problem, scope, outcomes, risk framing and priority opportunities.

Most importantly, we should be able to answer:

If the answer is partial or no, the team should identify the specific gaps, decisions or evidence needed before moving forward.
