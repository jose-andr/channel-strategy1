## Purpose of this phase

The Define phase turns Intelligent Front Door discovery evidence into a specific, evidence-based customer problem and prioritised opportunity that is ready to progress into Design.

Discovery narrowed the work from broad organisational and channel issues into recurring customer interaction patterns and priority service journeys.

Define now needs to go deeper.

The focus is to understand where the current front-door experience creates unnecessary customer effort, unclear routing, missing context or avoidable contact, and identify the first opportunity worth designing around.

The goal is not to redesign the full customer journey or define a solution.

The goal is to answer:

**What is the first clearly defined Intelligent Front Door opportunity we should take into Design?**

## Define approach

### Step 1 — Simplify the visual

Create a simpler Intelligent Front Door journey view that stakeholders can understand quickly.

The visual should make it easy to discuss:

- what the customer is trying to do;
- where they enter;
- what information or support they receive;
- where they are routed;
- where context may be lost;
- where avoidable effort or contact occurs; and
- where an Intelligent Front Door could improve the experience.

The visual is a working tool, not a final service blueprint.

### Step 2 — Dry-run one journey

The core team will test the approach on one priority journey before wider stakeholder use.

The dry run should test:

- whether the visual is understandable;
- whether it helps expose specific problems;
- which information is missing;
- where assumptions are being made;
- whether useful opportunity statements emerge; and
- what needs to change before stakeholder sessions.

### Step 3 — Validate with stakeholders

Relevant frontline, service and design stakeholders will be brought into focused Define sessions.

The sessions should test:

- whether the current understanding reflects actual practice;
- where operational exceptions or workarounds exist;
- which customer problems occur repeatedly;
- where customers experience unnecessary effort;
- where better information, routing, support or continuity could create value; and
- which assumptions still require evidence.

### Step 4 — Define opportunities

For each selected journey, develop a consistent problem/opportunity statement.

### Step 5 — Prioritise

Compare the opportunities and select the strongest candidate, or justified combination, to progress into Design.

What should become easier, clearer or more connected?

## Design principles

These principles should guide the transition from Define into Design.

| Principle | What it means for this deliverable |
| --- | --- |
| Customer value first | Prioritise improvements that materially reduce customer effort or improve clarity |
| Equity and accessibility by design | Do not optimise the front door in a way that removes appropriate assisted pathways |
| Reuse before reinvention | Look for reusable service logic rather than journey-specific fixes only |
| Minimum viable before enterprise-wide | Prove useful Connected Interaction patterns through selected journeys before scaling |
| Evidence before preference | Use Discovery evidence and validation rather than stakeholder preference alone |
| Connected interactions over channel optimisation | Improve how channels and service pathways work together rather than optimising one channel in isolation |
| Plain language and clear pathways | Customers should understand what they can do and what happens next |
| Safe and compliant by design | Privacy, records, security, accessibility and service obligations must be considered as the work develops |

## Final Define outcome

By the end of Define, the team should not simply have more analysis.
