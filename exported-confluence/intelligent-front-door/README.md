## Purpose

The Intelligent Front Door deliverable will help City of Melbourne understand how customers should be guided to the right service, support mode or channel when they need to complete a task, find information, request help or resolve an issue.

For FY25/26, this work will focus on creating the evidence, service logic and reusable patterns needed to improve how customers enter, move through and are supported across City of Melbourne channels.

This is not about creating one single digital entry point for every customer need. It is about making the existing entry points work together more clearly, consistently and efficiently.

---

## Plain-English summary

Customers currently reach City of Melbourne through many different front doors, including Google, the website, Contact Us, phone, forms, payments, report and request pathways, Digital Assistant, email, service teams and partner channels.

The Intelligent Front Door work will help clarify:

- what customers are trying to do
- why they need support
- where they start
- where they get sent
- where they get stuck
- which channel or support mode is most appropriate
- what information, routing or service logic is needed to improve the experience

The outcome will support clearer pathways for customers and better handoffs between digital, phone, assisted and specialist support.

---

## Why this matters in FY25/26

Channel Strategy Y2 is focused on moving from fragmented customer channels to connected customer interactions.

The Intelligent Front Door is one of the practical ways to make this shift.

It will help the organisation move away from improving channels in isolation and towards designing clearer entry, routing and support patterns around customer intent.

This matters because customers do not think in channels. They think in tasks, needs and outcomes.

## What this deliverable is not

This deliverable is not:

- a full enterprise-wide front door transformation
- a technology procurement process
- a full redesign of every City of Melbourne service
- a replacement for the broader Channel Strategy Y2 work
- a website-only or chatbot-only project
- a decision to force customers into digital channels
- a full operating model redesign

The work may identify technology, governance, data or operating model implications, but these will be framed as findings, decisions or future opportunities.

## Key risks

| Risk | Impact | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The work is treated as a technology solution too early | Root causes may be missed | Frame Intelligent Front Door as a routing, service logic and support model problem first |
| Data is incomplete or inconsistent | Prioritisation may be challenged | Use multiple sources and record confidence levels |
| The scope becomes too broad | Discovery may lose focus | Limit deep analysis to priority services and contact reasons |
| The work is seen as digital-only | Phone, assisted and specialist needs may be underrepresented | Include cross-channel evidence and non-digital support needs |
| Stakeholders expect immediate implementation | Discovery may be rushed | Use the 4D structure to show what must be known before moving forward |
| Service owners are not engaged early enough | Findings may not reflect operational reality | Validate current-state flows and constraints with service owners |
