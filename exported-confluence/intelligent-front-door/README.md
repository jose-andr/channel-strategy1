## Purpose

The Intelligent Front Door deliverable helps City of Melbourne understand how customers should be guided to the right information, service, support mode or channel when they need to complete a task, find information, request help or resolve an issue.

The work is not about creating one universal digital front door.

It is about making customer entry points work together more clearly by understanding:

- what the customer is trying to achieve
- what they need to know
- where they enter
- what service logic should guide them
- when self-service is appropriate
- when assisted or specialist support is required
- how the interaction should connect to the next step

---

## Plain-English summary

Customers reach City of Melbourne through many different front doors.

These include search engines, the website, forms, Contact Us, phone, Digital Assistant, email, service teams and other pathways.

A customer should not need to understand City of Melbourne's internal structure to know where to start.

The Intelligent Front Door work is therefore focused on creating reusable logic for:

1. understanding customer intent
2. identifying the appropriate service or information
3. determining the right response or support mode
4. routing the customer to the appropriate pathway
5. preserving context where the interaction moves between channels

## Sustainability proof of concept

### Why sustainability is useful

The sustainability work provides an opportunity to test Intelligent Front Door principles while the service experience is still being shaped.

Rather than beginning with a fixed technology solution, the proof of concept should explore the service logic required to help customers answer questions such as:

- What sustainability information or service am I looking for?
- What am I trying to achieve?
- Can I find the answer directly?
- Do I need to take an action?
- Do I need specialist support?
- What is the next appropriate pathway?

### What the proof of concept should test

| Test area | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Customer intent | Can we organise the experience around what the customer is trying to achieve? |
| Entry | Can a customer understand where to begin? |
| Knowledge | Can the right information be surfaced clearly and consistently? |
| Routing | Can the experience guide a customer toward the appropriate next action? |
| Support mode | Can the experience distinguish between information, self-service, assisted and specialist needs? |
| UX | Can the interaction be simple and consistent with the wider digital experience? |
| Reusability | Does the underlying logic provide patterns that could be used by other services? |

### Interaction concepts to explore

Potential interaction patterns discussed include:

- a search-style entry
- structured questions based on customer intent
- guided knowledge retrieval
- an agentic or conversational interaction
- clearer routing to an existing service pathway

These are **concepts to explore**, not agreed technical solutions.

The proof of concept should select the simplest interaction that allows the service logic to be tested meaningfully.

## Parking / infringement review learning

The parking / infringement review work remains an important related design case.

Current learning suggests value in:

- clarifying customer eligibility or review criteria earlier
- improving routing based on those criteria
- reducing uncertainty before a customer starts the process
- translating policy or operational rules into clearer customer guidance

The immediate task is to refine and validate the existing problem statement so that it is ready for Design.

The sustainability proof of concept should be used as an additional source of learning rather than as a replacement for this work.

## What this deliverable is not

This deliverable is not:

- a full enterprise front-door transformation
- a commitment to a chatbot
- a commitment to an AI agent
- a website-only initiative
- a technology procurement exercise
- a redesign of every City of Melbourne service
- a strategy to force customers into digital channels
- a replacement for service-owner accountability
- a full operating-model redesign

Technology may enable future patterns, but the work should define the customer and service logic first.

## Key risks

| Risk | Impact | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The proof of concept becomes technology-led | The team may test a tool rather than the customer problem | Define the service logic and learning questions first |
| Sustainability becomes a one-off prototype | Learning may not contribute to Channel Strategy Y2 | Explicitly capture reusable patterns and design implications |

| Digital pathways are prioritised without assisted support | Some customer needs may be excluded | Include support-mode and escalation logic |
| Design moves ahead without validated service rules | The experience may route customers incorrectly | Validate service rules with owners before relying on them |
| Evidence remains fragmented | Priority decisions may be challenged | Record evidence, assumptions and confidence clearly |
