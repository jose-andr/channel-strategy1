## Purpose of this discovery phase

The purpose of this discovery phase is to test whether we know enough to move forward.

Discovery will establish the evidence base for the Intelligent Front Door deliverable. It will help City of Melbourne understand the highest-priority services, the main reasons customers need support, and how customers currently move across channels when trying to complete a task or resolve an issue.

The goal is not to prove a preferred solution. The goal is to understand whether there is enough evidence to confidently move into Define.

---

## Core discovery question

**Do we know enough about customer demand, support needs and current channel movement to define the right Intelligent Front Door opportunities?**

Supporting question:

**What are customers trying to do, why do they need support, and where do they currently end up across City of Melbourne channels?**

## Why this matters

Customers currently experience City of Melbourne through multiple entry points, including search, website, Contact Us, phone, forms, payments, reports, requests, Digital Assistant and service teams.

Without a shared understanding of customer demand and contact reasons, channel improvements risk being based on internal assumptions rather than real customer needs.

This discovery creates the evidence needed to design clearer routing, better service pathways and more consistent support across channels.

It also helps avoid jumping too quickly to a technology solution before understanding the customer need, service complexity and operational constraints.

## Interaction mode model

CFT-2716 refined the original support-mode categories into a two-level model.

The primary interaction modes are:

**Routine → Guided → Assisted**

These describe the level of support, judgement, context and continuity required by the interaction.

Channel is treated separately.

A digital interaction is not automatically Routine, and a phone interaction is not automatically Assisted.

### Interaction modes

| Interaction mode | Description | Typical support patterns |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Routine** | Standard, predictable and repeatable interactions with low ambiguity and low support need | Inform, Transact, Track, Report |
| **Guided** | Interactions where the customer needs help identifying the right pathway, understanding eligibility, diagnosing the need or navigating the next step | Identify, Diagnose, Assess eligibility, Navigate, Complete with help |
| **Assisted** | Interactions requiring tailored support, specialist judgement, sensitivity, coordination, exception handling or continuity | Resolve, Specialist, Sensitive, Coordinated, Exception |

### How the original CFT-2716 categories were refined

| Original category | Refined interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| **Self-service** | Most commonly Routine — Inform / Transact / Track / Report |
| **Guided self-service** | Guided — Identify / Diagnose / Assess eligibility / Navigate |
| **Assisted digital** | Guided — Complete with help, or Assisted where judgement or continuity is required |
| **Phone support** | Delivery channel rather than an interaction mode |
| **Specialist support** | Assisted — Specialist |
| **Exception handling** | Assisted — Exception |

### Classification rule

Interaction mode should be assigned at **task level**, not simply at service or contact-reason level.

CFT-2716 found that the same broad customer reason can require different support levels.

For example:

**Apply**

- Parking Permit Application → Routine
- Construction Permit → Guided
- Infringement Review → Assisted

**Report an Issue**

- standard graffiti report → Routine
- dumped rubbish investigation → Guided
- rapid-response issue → Assisted

Accessibility, language, non-digital needs, vulnerability, failed self-service, urgency and exceptional circumstances may change the required support level without changing the underlying customer intent.

For the detailed criteria, priority-service mappings, confidence levels and evidence, see:

**Support mode model and priority service mapping**

## Priority interaction mode summary

The detailed task-level mapping is maintained in the **Support mode model and priority service mapping** artefact.

CFT-2716 used priority-service evidence to test the interaction-mode model against real customer tasks.

### Strong Routine patterns

Examples include:

- Parking Permit Application
- Parking Infringement Nomination
- standard parking information
- Graffiti Removal reporting
- Ratepayer Waste reporting
- standard infrastructure reporting
- rates notice retrieval
- balance / payment confirmation

Typical opportunity:

**Answer → Transact → Report → Confirm**

### Strong Guided patterns

Examples include:

- Disabled / Medical Parking enquiries
- Reserved Parking enquiries
- Construction Permit applications
- dumped rubbish investigation
- account-detail changes
- Land Information Certificate enquiries
- conditional waiver / refund pathways

Typical opportunity:

**Understand → Ask → Assess → Guide → Route**

### Strong Assisted patterns

Examples include:

- Parking Infringement Reviews
- high-priority Parking Enforcement
- rapid-response dumped rubbish
- rates valuation enquiries
- sensitive or safety-related infrastructure issues

Typical opportunity:

**Recognise → Preserve context → Connect → Maintain continuity**

### Key finding

**Contact Reason ≠ Interaction Mode**

Interaction mode needs to be determined from the characteristics of the specific customer task, including:

- predictability
- complexity
- support need
- sensitivity / risk
- context required
- continuity / ownership

## Evidence confidence scale

Use this scale to separate what we know from what we assume.

✅ Do we know where customer demand comes from?

✅ Do we know the highest-volume services?

✅ Do we know why customers contact us?

✅ Do we understand where our evidence is weak or missing?

✅ Do we know which evidence gaps must be addressed before designing the Intelligent Front Door?

| Confidence | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| High | Supported by reliable data from more than one source, or validated by data and service owner input |
| Medium | Supported by one reliable source or consistent stakeholder evidence, but not fully validated |
| Low | Based on limited evidence, anecdotal input or early assumptions |
| Unknown | Data is missing or not yet available |

## What Discovery tells us

### 1. The problem is not primarily digital adoption

Customers are already attempting to use digital channels for many high-volume interactions.

The recurring challenge is whether they can successfully:

**find**  
→ **understand**  
→ **complete**  
→ **confirm**  
→ **get help when needed**

Channel Strategy analysis reinforces that customers frequently leave digital pathways when information is difficult to find, processes are unclear or a journey fails.

---

### 2. Search and navigation are part of the interaction

Customers often begin with:

- Google
- website search
- a notice or direct link
- a customer-language question

rather than an internal City of Melbourne service name.

General Enquiries is therefore acting partly as a navigation and discovery layer when customers cannot identify the correct pathway.

---

### 3. The same service can contain very different support needs

Interaction mode cannot be assigned at service or channel level alone.

Priority evidence demonstrates that individual tasks can be:

**Routine**  
→ predictable, low-support information or completion

**Guided**  
→ requires eligibility, diagnosis, navigation or decision support

**Assisted**  
→ requires judgement, sensitivity, specialist expertise or continuity

Channel remains separate from interaction mode.

---

### 4. Failed self-service should not be confused with complex service need

A customer using phone or assisted support does not automatically have an Assisted interaction.

Some customers move into support because:

- a form failed
- a pathway was unclear
- information was missing
- they could not find the correct action
- status or confirmation was unavailable

Define should distinguish **service complexity** from **pathway failure**.

---

### 5. Handoffs matter when context or ownership is lost

Movement between channels or teams is not inherently a failure.

The design problem is whether customers can move without unnecessary:

- repetition
- re-diagnosis
- loss of evidence
- loss of status
- unclear ownership
- inconsistent advice

For Guided and Assisted interactions, continuity becomes increasingly important.

---

### 6. Better self-service and better assisted support are complementary

Rates customer research reinforces that customers value:

- clear information
- control
- safe digital access
- consistent experiences
- access to support when needed

The implication is not digital-only service.

It is to make Routine interactions easier while preserving appropriate Guided and Assisted pathways.

## Priority problem areas for Define

Define should begin with the following problem areas:

1. **Intent and pathway recognition**  
   Help customers reach the correct task without requiring them to understand internal service structures.
2. **Routine information and completion**  
   Make simple information, transactions, tracking and reporting easier to complete.
3. **Guided decision support**  
   Help customers with eligibility, evidence, diagnosis and pathway choice.
4. **Failed self-service recovery**  
   Allow customers to get help without restarting or repeating information.
5. **Status and next action**  
   Provide clearer confirmation, progress, timing and next-step information.
6. **Context-preserving handoff**  
   Determine what minimum context should move across channels and teams.
7. **Assisted and exception recognition**  
   Identify when specialist, sensitive, urgent or exception handling is required.
8. **Connected interaction standards**  
   Define what should remain consistent when customers move between channels.
9. **Inclusive access**  
   Ensure accessibility, language and non-digital support remain part of the core interaction model.

---

## Define should not assume the solution

Discovery does **not** provide evidence to automatically conclude that the answer is:

- a new channel
- a new platform
- an AI assistant
- an IVR replacement
- a chatbot
- additional content
- a redesigned form
- a new CRM workflow

Current evidence suggests some high-volume customer effort may first be reduced through improvements to:

- search
- navigation
- findability
- pathway clarity
- authoritative information
- form completion
- confirmation
- status visibility

More advanced intent recognition, orchestration, automation or AI should be introduced only where they address a validated customer or operational problem.
