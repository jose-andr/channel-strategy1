## Purpose of this phase

The Design phase creates the future-state Information Architecture approach, patterns, standards and artefacts needed to make priority service pathways easier to find, understand and use across channels.

The goal is to move from an agreed IA problem and scope into a practical, testable approach for naming, grouping, channel offers, routing cues and escalation pathways.

This phase should answer:

**Do we have a testable future-state IA approach that helps customers find, understand and use the right service pathway across channels?**

---

## Design question

**What should the future service information architecture look like, and how will naming, labels, channel offers, routing and escalation work across website, Contact Us, IVR, Digital Assistant, Contact Centre, knowledge and reporting structures?**

## Naming and label standards

Use this section to define the proposed naming standards for priority services.

| Standard | What it means | Example / notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Use customer task language | Labels should start from what the customer is trying to do | Pay, apply, report, request, check, get help |
| Avoid internal team names | Do not use business unit names unless customers know them | Use “Report graffiti” instead of internal program name |
| Use one recognisable service name | The same service should be recognisable across channels | Website, IVR and Digital Assistant should not use unrelated labels |
| Make the action clear | Customers should know what will happen next | “Apply for a permit” is clearer than “Permit services” |
| Use formal terms only where needed | Legal or policy terms can appear after a plain-English label | “Footpath trading permit” with explanation |
| Show help pathways | Customers should know where to go if they cannot self-serve | “Need help? Contact us…” |
| Map labels to reporting | Customer labels should be connected to internal categories | Maintain label-to-code mapping |

## Channel role design

| Channel | Future IA role | Key requirements | Risks / considerations |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Website | Primary public information and transaction pathway for many services | Clear labels, headings, task pathways and support cues | Risk of becoming the only assumed source for all needs |
| Search | Entry point that should connect customers to the right service label or task | Metadata, headings and customer-language labels | Search results may expose old or inconsistent labels |
| Contact Us | Assisted routing and enquiry pathway | Categories aligned to customer task and service language | Misaligned categories may increase misrouting |
| Digital Assistant | Guided support and answer pathway for common intents | Intents aligned to customer language and approved knowledge | Bot labels may drift from service labels |
| IVR / phone | Assisted access and routing pathway | Plain-language menu terms and clear escalation | Menu limits may constrain label length |
| Contact Centre | Human support, triage and escalation pathway | Staff mapping between customer terms and service pathways | Staff may need guidance when labels change |
| Knowledge | Source-of-truth support for labels, definitions and escalation rules | Knowledge taxonomy aligned to service IA | Inconsistent knowledge may undermine IA |
| CRM / reporting | Internal categorisation and tracking | Label-to-code mapping | Reporting continuity may be affected by changes |
| Service teams | Specialist advice and resolution pathways | Clear escalation labels and ownership | Service constraints may limit immediate changes |

## Controls and compliance considerations

| Area | Consideration | Required action | Owner |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Privacy | Some pathways may involve personal or sensitive information | Ensure secure and assisted pathways are clear where needed |  |
| Consent | Some support pathways may require customer consent before action | Identify where consent cues are needed |  |
| Records | IA decisions and label changes may need traceability | Maintain decision log and label mapping record |  |
| Accessibility | Labels and navigation must support disability, low literacy and assistive technology needs | Complete accessibility and plain English checks |  |
| Language and inclusion | Customer language may vary across communities and language groups | Consider translated, plain English and assisted alternatives |  |
| Information security | Some staff-only pathways or restricted information may not be public | Separate public labels from staff-only routing where required |  |
| Data governance | Label changes may affect reporting and analytics | Maintain mapping between old labels, new labels and internal codes |  |
| Legal / policy | Some formal terms may be required | Pair formal terminology with customer-friendly explanations |  |

## Design exit criteria

Design can move into Deliver when:

- the future-state IA concept is clear and linked to the defined problem
- priority service labels and task labels are proposed
- channel offer model is documented
- label alignment map is created for priority channels
- customer-facing labels are mapped to internal service codes, categories or reporting structures
- current-state fixes are separated from future-state or dependent changes
- reusable IA patterns are documented
- staff, service team and channel owner impacts are visible
- content, knowledge and data needs are documented
- accessibility, plain language and inclusion considerations are visible
- key assumptions have a test plan or have been tested
- success measures are ready to be tracked
- there is enough detail to pilot, test, implement or hand over

## Final Design outcome

By the end of Design, the team should have a clear, practical and testable future-state Information Architecture approach.

Most importantly, we should be able to answer:

**Do we have a testable future-state IA approach that helps customers find, understand and use the right service pathway across channels?**

If the answer is yes, the work can progress into Deliver.

If the answer is partial or no, the team should identify the specific design gaps, risks or decisions that need to be resolved before moving forward.
