## 1.2. Purpose

The Information Architecture deliverable will help City of Melbourne understand whether priority services and support pathways are named, organised and presented consistently across customer channels.

For FY25/26, this work will focus on high-demand services and contact reasons identified through Intelligent Front Door discovery.

The goal is to create practical service information architecture foundations that make it easier for customers to find the right pathway, understand what action to take and know where to get help.

---

## 1.3. Plain-English summary

Customers do not experience City of Melbourne through organisational structures. They come with tasks, needs and questions.

If the same service is named differently across the website, Contact Us, IVR, Digital Assistant and Contact Centre, customers may:

- choose the wrong pathway
- abandon the task
- call for help
- repeat themselves
- receive inconsistent guidance
- be routed to the wrong team

The Information Architecture work will help clarify:

- how priority services are currently named across channels
- whether service labels match customer language
- whether customers can understand what each channel offers
- where labels, groupings and prompts are inconsistent
- where customers may be misrouted or confused
- what should be standardised first
- which changes are feasible now versus future-state improvements

This is not just a website navigation exercise. It is a cross-channel service IA activity.

---

## 1.4. Why this matters in FY25/26

Channel Strategy Y2 is focused on moving from fragmented customer channels to connected customer interactions.

A shared service information architecture is one of the foundations for that shift.

If channels use different names, categories or prompts for the same service, connected interactions become harder to deliver. Customers may not recognise the right pathway, staff may use different terminology, and reporting may become difficult to compare across channels.

This deliverable will support clearer customer pathways across:

- website
- Contact Us
- Digital Assistant
- IVR
- Contact Centre
- key transaction pathways
- service owner guidance
- data and reporting structures

## 1.8. What this deliverable is not

This deliverable is not:

- a full website redesign
- a full IVR redesign
- a full chatbot redesign
- a brand strategy project
- a full enterprise taxonomy redesign
- a full content rewrite
- a replacement for service owner accountability
- a technology-only routing solution

The work may identify website, IVR, Digital Assistant, Contact Us, content, reporting or taxonomy implications, but these will be framed as findings, decisions or future opportunities.

## 1.11. Key risks

| Risk | Impact | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| IA is assessed from an internal perspective | Recommendations may not improve customer navigation | Use customer search terms and contact reasons as the anchor |
| Channel owners optimise separately | Inconsistency may continue across channels | Compare channels side by side |
| Naming changes create reporting issues | Data continuity may be affected | Map customer-facing labels to internal service codes |
| Legal or policy terms are required | Plain English may be constrained | Use customer-friendly labels with formal terms where needed |
| Scope expands to whole-of-site IA | Discovery may become too large | Limit deep assessment to priority journeys |
| Future-state ideas are mistaken for immediate fixes | Delivery expectations may become unrealistic | Clearly separate current-state findings from future-state opportunities |
| IA changes are made without governance | Inconsistency may return over time | Recommend minimum viable naming and IA governance |
