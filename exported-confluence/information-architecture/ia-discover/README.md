## Purpose of this discovery phase

The purpose of this discovery phase is to test whether we know enough to move forward.

Discovery will assess whether high-demand services and support pathways are named, organised and presented consistently across customer channels.

It will use Intelligent Front Door discovery findings to assess whether customers can understand and navigate service offers across the website, Contact Us, phone IVR, Digital Assistant and key transaction pathways.

The goal is not to redesign every channel. The goal is to understand whether there is enough evidence to confidently move into Define.

---

## Core discovery question

**Do we know enough about how priority services and support pathways are named, organised and presented across channels to define the right Information Architecture opportunities?**

Supporting question:

**Are priority services and support pathways named and organised consistently across channels in a way customers understand?**

## Why this matters

Customers do not experience City of Melbourne through organisational structures. They come with tasks, needs and questions.

If the same service is named differently across website, IVR, Contact Us and Digital Assistant, customers may choose the wrong pathway, abandon the task, call for help or repeat themselves across channels.

This discovery will identify where language, labels, grouping and routing need to be standardised to support connected customer interactions.

It also helps avoid treating IA as a website-only problem, when the customer experience spans multiple front doors and support channels.

## IA assessment criteria

| Criterion | Assessment question |
| --- | --- |
| Customer language | Does the label match how customers describe the need? |
| Consistency | Is the same service named consistently across channels? |
| Findability | Can customers easily locate the service or task? |
| Task clarity | Is it clear what action the customer can take? |
| Channel clarity | Is it clear which channel should be used for which need? |
| Completeness | Does the channel offer enough information to complete or progress the task? |
| Accessibility | Is the structure usable for people with disability, low literacy or language needs? |
| Escalation clarity | Is it clear when and how customers can get help? |
| Data alignment | Do service names align with reporting and analytics structures? |
| Maintainability | Can the IA be managed and updated sustainably? |
| Feasibility | Is the change practical within current systems, ownership and delivery constraints? |

## Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Impact | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| IA is assessed from an internal perspective | Recommendations may not improve customer navigation | Use customer search terms and contact reasons as the anchor |
| Channel owners optimise separately | Inconsistency may continue | Compare channels side by side |
| Naming changes create reporting issues | Data continuity may be affected | Map customer-facing labels to internal service codes |
| Legal or policy terms are required | Plain English may be constrained | Use customer-friendly labels with formal terms where needed |
| Scope expands to whole-of-site IA | Discovery may become too large | Limit deep assessment to priority journeys |
| Future-state opportunities are treated as quick fixes | Delivery expectations may become unrealistic | Separate Now / Later / Dependent opportunities |
| Accessibility and language needs are missed | IA may work for some customers but not all | Include accessibility, language and assisted pathway considerations |

## Discovery exit criteria

Discovery can move into Define when:

- priority journeys are selected from Intelligent Front Door demand evidence
- channel offers are mapped for the priority journeys
- naming differences across website, Contact Us, IVR and Digital Assistant are documented
- customer language gaps are identified
- IA issues are assessed against agreed criteria
- current-state fixes are separated from future-state or dependent opportunities
- accessibility, language and non-digital needs have been considered
- data and reporting impacts are visible
- evidence gaps and assumptions are documented
- priority IA opportunities can be recommended for Define
- unresolved decisions are clear enough to take into Define

## Final discovery outcome

By the end of this activity, we should know where priority services and contact reasons are inconsistently named, organised or routed across channels.

We should also know which information architecture improvements should be prioritised to reduce confusion, avoidable contact and wrong-pathway selection.

Most importantly, we should be able to answer:

**Do we know enough to move forward into Define?**

If the answer is yes, the work can progress into problem framing, MVP IA opportunity selection and future-state IA principles.

If the answer is partial or no, the team should identify the specific evidence gaps, naming conflicts, feasibility issues or decisions that need to be resolved before moving forward.
