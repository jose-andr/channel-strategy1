## Purpose

This page records the important decisions made during Channel Strategy Y2.

The log exists to make clear:

- what has been decided
- who made the decision
- why it was made
- what the decision affects
- what still needs to be resolved
- what follow-up is required

Channel Strategy Y2 involves connected work across CX, Digital, Contact Centre, CRM, Knowledge, Information Architecture, Data, Communications and service teams. Decisions need to remain visible so related work does not drift into separate channel solutions.

---

## Decision principles

| Principle | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Customer value first | Decisions should improve customer clarity, effort, continuity or confidence |
| Connected interactions over isolated channel optimisation | Avoid improving one channel in ways that make the end-to-end journey worse |
| Test before scaling | Use practical services and journeys to learn before defining enterprise-wide patterns |
| Reuse before reinvention | Prefer patterns and decision logic that can work across multiple services |
| Evidence before preference | Use customer demand, effort, contact reasons, operational evidence and feasibility |
| Human in the loop | Technology can support routing, knowledge and interaction but people remain accountable for decisions |
| Maintain assisted access | Digital improvements must continue to provide appropriate support pathways |
| Make uncertainty visible | Distinguish confirmed decisions from hypotheses, experiments and unresolved questions |
| Clear ownership | Each material decision should have an accountable owner and next step |

## Decision status definitions

| Status | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Proposed | A decision has been identified but has not yet been agreed |
| Under review | Options or evidence are still being considered |
| Approved | The decision has been made and can be acted on |
| Deferred | The decision or activity has been deliberately paused |
| Rejected | The option was considered and not accepted |
| Superseded | A later decision has replaced it |

## Escalation guidance

Escalate when a decision:

- changes agreed scope or value drops
- materially affects another team or program
- requires technology investment or platform change
- introduces privacy, records, security or data governance implications
- changes a public-facing customer commitment
- affects accessibility or assisted access
- requires material funding or organisational prioritisation

## Decision quality check

A decision is considered sufficiently recorded when:

1. the decision is stated clearly
2. the decision maker is known
3. the rationale is visible
4. implications are understood
5. follow-up is assigned where required
6. relevant stakeholders have been informed
