## Purpose of this phase

The Define phase turns Knowledge Management System discovery evidence into a clear problem frame, agreed scope, success measures and priority opportunities.

The goal is to make sure the team understands the knowledge problem before moving into Design.

This phase should answer:

**Do we have the right knowledge problem, scope, governance direction and success measures to move forward into Design?**

---

## Define question

**What knowledge problem are we solving, for whom, and what needs to be true for City of Melbourne to provide consistent, accurate and reusable answers across channels?**

---

## What “ready to move forward” means

Define is complete enough to move into Design when we can answer the following questions with reasonable confidence.

| Readiness question | What good looks like |
| --- | --- |
| Do we have a clear knowledge problem statement? | The problem is written in plain English and supported by discovery evidence |
| Do we know who the knowledge system needs to support? | Customer, staff, service owner, Contact Centre, Digital Assistant, website and CRM needs are clearly identified |
| Do we know what is in and out of scope? | Scope is specific enough to guide knowledge model, governance and platform decisions |
| Do we know which knowledge gaps matter most? | Priority gaps are selected using evidence, risk and channel impact |
| Do we know what good knowledge looks like? | Success measures and quality criteria are defined |
| Do we know the governance direction? | Ownership, review, approval and assurance needs are clear enough to design |
| Do we know the key constraints? | Privacy, records, accessibility, information security, service ownership, technology and capacity constraints are visible |
| Do we know what decisions are needed? | Open decisions are documented with owners |
| Do we have enough to start designing? | The team can move into future-state knowledge model, taxonomy, governance and KMS option design |

## Knowledge principles

These principles should guide the next stage of work.

| Principle | What it means for Knowledge Management System |
| --- | --- |
| Single source of truth | Each priority customer question should have one approved answer that can be reused across channels |
| Clear ownership | Knowledge must have a named business owner and approver |
| Current and versioned | Knowledge should have review dates, change history and clear version control |
| Reusable across channels | Knowledge should support website, Contact Centre, Digital Assistant, IVR, CRM and staff use where appropriate |
| Customer language first | Knowledge should be structured around customer tasks and questions, not only internal business units |
| Accessible and inclusive | Knowledge should support plain English, accessibility, language needs and assisted pathways |
| Safe and compliant by design | Privacy, records, information security and legal considerations should be built in early |
| AI-ready only when governed | AI-assisted retrieval should only use approved, current, structured and low-risk knowledge |
| Minimum viable before enterprise-wide | Start with priority services and reusable patterns before scaling across the organisation |

## Constraints

| Constraint | Impact | Design implication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Policy / regulatory | Some answers may require formal wording or specialist review | Use plain English summaries with links to formal terms where needed |
| Privacy / consent | Some knowledge may involve personal or sensitive information | Define safe boundaries and escalation rules |
| Records | Approvals, changes and versions may need to be retained | Include auditability and version control in the knowledge model |
| Accessibility | Knowledge must be usable for people with disability, low literacy or language needs | Design for plain English, structure and assisted pathways |
| Information security | Internal procedures or sensitive guidance may not be suitable for all channels | Separate public, staff-only and restricted knowledge where required |
| Technology | Current platforms may not support all desired knowledge features | Define minimum viable requirements and transition path |
| Data quality | Knowledge metadata, ownership and review dates may be incomplete | Include confidence levels and data gaps in design |
| Operational capacity | Service owners may have limited time to maintain content | Design lightweight governance and prioritise high-risk / high-demand knowledge |
| Governance | Too much governance may slow updates; too little may reduce trust | Define minimum viable governance with clear decision rights |

## Define exit criteria

Define can move into Design when:

- the knowledge problem statement is clear and evidence-based
- priority knowledge users and needs are agreed
- in-scope and out-of-scope items are documented
- priority knowledge opportunities are selected
- success measures and knowledge quality criteria are defined
- minimum viable knowledge taxonomy needs are clear
- source-of-truth and governance direction is agreed enough to design
- KMS option direction or shortlist is clear
- AI-readiness requirements are defined
- privacy, records, accessibility and information security constraints are visible
- open decisions have owners
- assumptions are documented
- there is enough clarity to create future-state knowledge model, governance, taxonomy and KMS patterns

## Final Define outcome

By the end of Define, the team should have a clear and agreed view of the Knowledge Management System problem, scope, outcomes, principles, governance direction and priority opportunities.

Most importantly, we should be able to answer:

**Do we have the right knowledge problem, scope, governance direction and success measures to move forward into Design?**

If the answer is yes, the work can progress into future-state knowledge model, taxonomy, governance and KMS design.

If the answer is partial or no, the team should identify the specific gaps, decisions or evidence needed before moving forward.
