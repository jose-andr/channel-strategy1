## Purpose

The Knowledge Management System deliverable will help City of Melbourne understand what knowledge structure, governance and system options are needed to provide consistent, accurate and reusable answers across customer channels.

For FY25/26, this work will focus on assessing current knowledge sources, taxonomy, governance, ownership and platform options against agreed knowledge principles.

This work supports Channel Strategy Y2 by making sure customer-facing and staff-facing channels can rely on trusted knowledge.

---

## Plain-English summary

Connected customer interactions depend on consistent answers.

Customers and staff currently rely on multiple sources of information, including website content, Contact Centre guidance, Digital Assistant responses, CRM notes, service owner procedures, scripts, FAQs and internal documents.

If these sources provide different answers, customers may receive inconsistent advice, repeat contact may increase, and staff may spend more time checking or correcting information.

The Knowledge Management System work will help clarify:

- where knowledge currently lives
- which sources are trusted
- who owns and approves knowledge
- how knowledge is reviewed and updated
- whether knowledge can be reused across channels
- whether knowledge is structured enough to support Digital Assistant, IVR and future AI-enabled experiences
- what governance is needed before scaling connected interactions

This is not just a technology question. A platform will only work if knowledge is owned, structured, current, accessible and governed.

---

## Why this matters in FY25/26

Channel Strategy Y2 is focused on moving from fragmented customer channels to connected customer interactions.

A shared knowledge foundation is essential to that shift.

The Intelligent Front Door, Digital Assistant, Contact Centre, website, IVR, CRM and future AI-supported experiences all depend on reliable knowledge.

If the organisation does not have a clear approach to knowledge ownership, taxonomy, review, approval and reuse, channel improvements may continue to produce inconsistent answers.

This deliverable will help identify what must be fixed or established before knowledge can safely support connected and AI-enabled customer interactions.

## What this deliverable is not

This deliverable is not:

- a final platform procurement process
- a full content rewrite
- a full enterprise data architecture project
- an AI deployment project
- a full policy review
- a replacement for existing service owner accountability
- a decision to centralise all knowledge ownership without consultation
- a technology-only solution

The work may identify platform, governance, content, taxonomy, AI-readiness or operating model implications, but these will be framed as findings, decisions or future opportunities.

## Key risks

| Risk | Impact | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Knowledge is treated as a technology issue only | Governance and ownership gaps may remain unresolved | Assess people, process, governance and platform together |
| No clear taxonomy exists | Knowledge may remain inconsistent across channels | Recommend a minimum viable taxonomy for priority services |
| Multiple sources provide different answers | Customers and staff may receive inconsistent advice | Identify source-of-truth ownership and approval pathways |
| Content is not AI-ready | AI tools may generate inaccurate or unsafe answers | Limit AI use to approved, structured and low-risk content |
| Service owners lack capacity to maintain content | Knowledge may become outdated | Recommend clear review cycles and lightweight governance |
| Governance becomes too heavy | Teams may avoid using or maintaining the knowledge base | Design minimum viable governance with clear roles |
| Internal and customer-facing knowledge are treated separately | Answers may remain inconsistent between staff and customers | Assess reuse across both staff and customer channels |
