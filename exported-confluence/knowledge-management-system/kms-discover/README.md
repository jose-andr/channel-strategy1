## Purpose of this discovery phase

The purpose of this discovery phase is to test whether we know enough to move forward.

Discovery will assess whether City of Melbourne has the knowledge foundations required to provide consistent, accurate and reusable answers across customer channels.

It will examine current knowledge sources, taxonomy, governance and platform options against the agreed knowledge principles.

The goal is not to select a platform immediately. The goal is to understand whether there is enough evidence to confidently move into Define.

---

## Core discovery question

**Do we know enough about knowledge structure, governance and system options to define the right Knowledge Management System direction?**

Supporting question:

**Do we have the right knowledge structure, governance and system options to provide consistent answers across channels?**

---

## What “ready to move forward” means

Discovery is complete enough to move into Define when we can answer the following questions with reasonable confidence.

| Readiness question | What good looks like |
| --- | --- |
| Do we know where knowledge currently lives? | Current knowledge sources are identified across website, Contact Centre, Digital Assistant, CRM, service teams and internal documents |
| Do we know which sources are trusted? | Source-of-truth arrangements are visible, even where incomplete |

## Why this matters

Connected customer interactions depend on consistent knowledge.

If website content, Contact Centre scripts, Digital Assistant responses, CRM notes and service owner guidance all provide different answers, customers and staff will continue to experience confusion, repeat contact and inconsistent service.

Before scaling Intelligent Front Door or AI-enabled support, City of Melbourne needs confidence that knowledge is accurate, owned, current, reusable and governed.

This discovery creates the evidence needed to define a practical knowledge direction for Channel Strategy Y2.

It also helps avoid jumping too quickly to a platform decision before understanding governance, ownership, taxonomy and content quality requirements.

## Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Impact | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Knowledge is treated as a technology issue only | Governance and ownership gaps may remain unresolved | Assess people, process, governance and platform together |
| No clear taxonomy exists | Knowledge may remain inconsistent across channels | Recommend a minimum viable taxonomy for priority services |
| Multiple sources provide different answers | Customers and staff receive inconsistent advice | Identify source-of-truth ownership and approval pathways |
| Content is not AI-ready | AI tools may generate inaccurate or unsafe answers | Limit AI use to approved, structured and low-risk content |
| Service owners lack capacity to maintain content | Knowledge may become outdated | Recommend clear review cycles and lightweight governance |
| Governance becomes too heavy | Teams may avoid using or maintaining knowledge | Recommend minimum viable governance with clear roles |
| Internal knowledge is not aligned to public content | Customers and staff may receive different answers | Test reuse across public and staff-facing channels |

## Discovery exit criteria

Discovery can move into Define when:

- current knowledge sources are identified and confidence-rated
- source-of-truth arrangements are understood or gaps are documented
- ownership, review and approval gaps are visible
- current taxonomy or taxonomy gaps are documented
- KMS options are assessed against agreed principles
- AI-readiness has been assessed using clear criteria
- privacy, records, accessibility and information security considerations have been included
- priority knowledge gaps or improvement areas are identified
- a preferred direction, shortlist or set of decisions can be recommended
- unresolved decisions are clear enough to take into Define

## Final discovery outcome

By the end of this activity, we should know whether City of Melbourne has the knowledge structure, governance and system options needed to support consistent answers across channels.

We should also know what must be improved before scaling Intelligent Front Door, Digital Assistant, IVR or future AI-enabled customer experiences.

Most importantly, we should be able to answer:

**Do we know enough to move forward into Define?**

If the answer is yes, the work can progress into problem framing, MVP knowledge use case selection and future-state knowledge principles.

If the answer is partial or no, the team should identify the specific evidence gaps, governance gaps, risks or decisions that need to be resolved before moving forward.
