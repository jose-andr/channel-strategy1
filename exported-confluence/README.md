## Summary

Channel Strategy Y2 is about helping City of Melbourne move from **fragmented customer channels** to **connected customer interactions**.

In Year 1, the work helped define the direction for customer channels. In Year 2, the focus shifts to practical enablement: creating reusable patterns, standards and decision tools that can be tested through real service journeys.

The work will define and test a minimum viable **Connected Interactions Framework**, supported by service information architecture, so customers can find the right pathway, receive the right level of support, move between channels with less repetition and receive updates they can understand.

---

## Why this matters

Customers do not experience Council as separate systems, teams or channels. They experience one organisation.

Today, customers may start on Google, move to the website, submit a request, call for an update, receive a system-generated status, and then speak to a staff member who may not have full context.

The opportunity is to create a shared way for City of Melbourne to design, route, support and measure customer interactions across channels.

| Current challenge | Y2 response |
| --- | --- |
| Channels are improved separately | Create shared service logic across channels |
| Customers search by intent, but services are often structured internally | Build intent-led service information architecture |
| Status updates may reflect internal workflow terms | Create customer-facing journey status patterns |
| Staff may need to reconstruct context | Define minimum data and handover needs |
| Digital, CRM, contact centre, knowledge and service work can drift | Align related initiatives through a common framework |
| Performance is often measured by channel | Shift toward journey-based measurement |

## What Y2 will produce

Channel Strategy Y2 will not be a standalone strategy document. It will produce practical assets that can be used by active initiatives.

| Output | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| **MVP Connected Interactions Framework** | A shared framework for designing connected customer interactions |
| **Response modes** | A minimum set of service response types to guide channel, support and routing decisions |
| **Service information architecture foundations** | A clearer way to organise services, support reasons and pathways around customer intent |
| **Journey-based status patterns** | Plain-English status updates that explain what has happened, what happens next and when to expect an update |
| **Routing and channel principles** | Decision rules for directing customers to the right pathway or support mode |
| **Minimum connected-context data needs** | The minimum information needed to support continuity, handover and measurement |
| **Journey measurement approach** | A way to measure customer experience across the end-to-end journey, not only by channel |

## Current focus areas

The work is organised around four discovery / enablement streams.

| --- | --- |

## Delivery principles

| Principle | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Start with high-demand journeys** | Focus where customer effort, volume or avoidable contact is highest |
| **Design for connected interactions** | Improve how channels work together, not just how each channel performs |
| **Use customer intent as the organising logic** | Structure pathways around what customers are trying to do |
| **Make service logic reusable** | Create patterns that can be applied across services |
| **Balance self-service with support access** | Improve digital pathways while protecting assisted options |
| **Measure journeys, not just channels** | Understand whether the customer need was resolved end to end |
| **Build governance and compliance in early** | Consider privacy, records, security, data quality and accessibility from the start |

## Plain-English narrative

Channel Strategy Y2 helps City of Melbourne create a more coherent customer experience across channels.

The work is not about forcing customers into digital channels or redesigning every service at once. It is about creating the practical service logic that helps customers find the right pathway, receive the right support, move between channels with less repetition and understand what is happening.

For staff and service teams, it creates clearer standards, better handover context, more consistent knowledge and a stronger way to measure experience across the full journey
